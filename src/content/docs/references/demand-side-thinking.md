---
title: Demand-side thinking
description: Circumstances, desired progress and the solutions that fit.
---

Ryan Singer describes learning from Bob Moesta and Chris Spiek to reverse the usual product conversation: from explaining a product’s features and benefits to understanding the world through the buyer’s eyes. A second reversal mattered just as much: people’s changing circumstances give them different kinds of progress to seek. [His account on JTBD Radio](https://jobstobedone.org/radio/ryan-singer-jtbd-radio/).

- **The demand side concerns the circumstances someone is in and the progress they seek.**
- **The supply side concerns what we can make to help them get there.**

Moesta treats these as interdependent perspectives: progress requires both a need and something capable of meeting it. [His explanation](https://therewiredgroup.com/circuit-breaker-podcast/unpacking-supply-and-demand/).

The takeaway for agent work:

**Understand what is prompting the change and what it should make possible before deciding its form.**

## Circumstances explain the choice

Singer recalls Moesta’s example of the same person wanting pizza one night and an expensive Italian restaurant another. Their identity has stayed the same; the situation and purpose have changed. A tiring day calls for relief from cooking. Another occasion calls for expressing affection or gratitude.

Asking what people want can produce an endless list. Singer instead looks at past situations where they struggled: what they tried, where they spent time and energy, and what succeeded or failed. Those choices reveal what mattered under actual constraints.

For agents, reconstruct the concrete difficulty behind a request: what made it matter, what the person has already tried, where that fell short, and what an improvement would let them do. Keep missing evidence visible.

## Let the problem change the solution

In Singer’s [calendar example](https://businessofsoftware.org/talks/framing-and-hard-conversations/), customers asked Basecamp for a calendar. Their existing agenda listed scheduled events but concealed available time. Understanding that difficulty made a small month view showing empty spaces sufficient. The word “calendar” alone could have implied a much larger product.

For agents, a feature name is a proposed solution. Recover the situation it must improve so that scope decisions preserve the useful effect.

## Move between the two views

Singer’s [“Shaping on the demand side”](https://www.ryansinger.co/shaping-on-the-demand-side/) describes integrating evidence about behaviour into an understanding of the problem that constrains a solution. Work can start on either side. A solution idea raises questions; research changes the idea; a prototype exposes another question.

For agents, connect each investigation to the decision its answer could change. Carry the understood problem into implementation so that technical tradeoffs remain connected to the person’s progress.
