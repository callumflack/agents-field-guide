## Suchman’s argument

An accounts payable clerk and supervisor start by looking for a missing payment. The clerk notices that an invoice in the paid file is page two. The packing slips and tax amount suggest that the apparently unpaid items belong on the missing first page of that invoice. The supervisor asks the clerk to get the page from the vendor.

Suchman argues that workers produce an orderly record through this kind of inquiry. The procedure says what the record must account for. The workers use documents, conversations and their knowledge of the office to decide how to establish it in this case. She proposes designing systems that help them do that work.

## Building with agents

Start with a real case where the records disagree. Watch what people check, which clue changes the question, and what they need before they can act.

During a Field Guide release, the changelog reported that two source links had been removed. The reference page still contained both. Comparing them showed that the changelog needed correcting. Make the conflicting records, the agent’s current explanation and the evidence still needed visible to whoever takes over.

Test whether the agent changes course when new evidence overturns its first explanation.
